*Chukwuma Theology | Sabbath Reflection | October 24, 2026*

It is easier to ask why people reject God’s message than to examine how we receive it ourselves. Today’s Sabbath School discussion brought that uncomfortable truth into focus: Is God’s Word actually transforming me?

We can recognize biblical truth, defend sound doctrine, and still resist the changes God asks us to make. Paul’s warning about having “a form of godliness” while denying its power becomes deeply personal here (2 Timothy 3:5). Faithfulness involves more than knowing what Scripture says. It requires allowing Scripture to shape our character.

Hebrews 1:1–3 gives this transformation its center. The God who spoke through the prophets has spoken through His Son. Jesus reveals God’s character with unsurpassed clarity. Every claim to speak for God must therefore be examined in the light of Scripture and the character of Christ.

Jesus embodied grace and truth. He confronted sin, attended to suffering, and called people toward restoration. His example challenges both our appetite for comforting messages and our tendency to confuse harshness with faithfulness. A message does not become true because it pleases us—or because it wounds us.

Isaiah described people who wanted to hear “smooth things” rather than unwelcome truth (Isaiah 30:10). That temptation remains familiar. We need discernment when a message consistently reassures us without ever calling us to repentance, humility, or costly obedience.

The discussion also reminded us that God equips His whole people for service. Joel’s promise includes sons and daughters, young and old, servants and maidservants (Joel 2:28–29). Although spiritual gifts differ, every believer has a responsibility to bear faithful witness to Christ. Those gifts serve the church’s growth toward unity and maturity (Ephesians 4:11–13).

This leaves us with a practical responsibility for the coming week: receive the Word prayerfully, cultivate the gifts God has entrusted to us, and let His grace govern how we treat one another.

Before asking whether someone else has accepted God’s message, I must ask whether I am living it.

Lord, make me receptive to Your Word, faithful in service, and more like Jesus in the way I speak and live. Amen.
