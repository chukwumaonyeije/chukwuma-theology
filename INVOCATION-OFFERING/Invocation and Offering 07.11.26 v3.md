# Invocation and Offering — July 11, 2026
### Communion Sabbath — Ephesians 2:14–19

---

## INVOCATION

Holy and Compassionate God,

We come to You this Sabbath morning as a gathered people — drawn from different homes, different histories, and different roads, yet seated together this morning under one roof, before one table. Some of us arrived early with hearts full of expectation; others walked in tired, carrying a week we haven't set down yet. Whatever road brought us here, we thank You that You have called every one of us to the same house.

Your Word tells us this morning, "For he himself is our peace, who has made the two groups one and has destroyed the barrier, the dividing wall of hostility" (Ephesians 2:14, NIV). Lord, Christ is not merely a peacemaker standing between two sides — He Himself is the peace. Long before we learned to make peace with one another, He made peace for us, at a cost only He could pay. We worship You for that.

Send Your Holy Spirit over this service, we pray — over the songs we will sing, the Word that will be opened, and the fellowship we will share. Let nothing about this hour feel routine. Let every part of it carry us nearer to the cross that tore down the wall.

We lift up before You this morning all who still feel like strangers — the visitor unsure if this is a place for them, the member who has quietly drifted to the edges, the ones who wonder whether they truly belong. Lord, let this congregation be living proof that in Christ there are no outsiders left. We ask for Your comfort upon the sick, the grieving, and the lonely, and for Your wisdom upon those who lead and shepherd this flock.

As we prepare our hearts to gather at Your table, remind us again what it cost to bring us near, and let this Sabbath rest be more than a pause from the week — let it be a homecoming.

In the matchless name of Jesus, our peace and our cornerstone, we pray. Amen.

## CALL FOR OFFERING

It is time for our tithes and offerings.

*[A suggested illustration — swap freely for a real memory if you have one, Chukwuma.]*

Some years ago I sat in the back of a courtroom on a Friday afternoon, waiting for a naturalization ceremony to begin. The room was full — nearly a hundred people from what must have been thirty different countries, dressed in their best clothes, some holding small flags, all of them holding the same folder of paperwork that had taken years, in some cases decades, to complete. A federal judge came in, and after a short address, asked everyone to stand and raise their right hand for the Oath of Allegiance. It took less than two minutes. And when it was finished, the judge said the words that changed everything: "Congratulations. You are now citizens of the United States." The room broke into tears and applause. People who had lived for years as resident aliens — legally present, but never fully belonging — were, in that instant, no longer strangers. They had full standing. Full rights. A permanent seat at the table of a nation that was, until that moment, not fully theirs.

That is the closest earthly picture I know of what Paul describes in our sermon text this morning. He writes: "He came and preached peace to you who were far away and peace to those who were near. For through him we both have access to the Father by one Spirit. Consequently, you are no longer foreigners and strangers, but fellow citizens with God's people and also members of his household" (Ephesians 2:17–19, NIV). No naturalization line, no years of paperwork, no oath we could recite on our own strength could have earned us that standing before God. It was granted the moment Christ tore down, in His own flesh, the wall that separated us from Him.

That is what makes this Communion Sabbath, and this offering, more than an appeal for funds. We do not give today to earn a place at God's table — Christ already purchased that for us, in full, at the cross. We give because the paperwork is already settled, the oath already sworn on our behalf, and citizenship in His household already granted. Our offering is simply gratitude finding a place to go.

**PRAYER FOR TITHES AND OFFERINGS**

Gracious Father, we thank You that in Christ we are no longer strangers but citizens, no longer outsiders but members of Your own household. As we return our tithes and bring our offerings this Communion Sabbath, receive them as the grateful response of a people who know they did not earn their place at Your table. Use what is given to carry that same welcome to others who still feel far away. In Jesus' name, we pray. Amen.

Thank you for your generous hearts. God bless you all.

## COMMUNION PRAYERS

**Prayer for the Bread**

Lord Jesus, we take this bread in remembrance of Your body, given so that the wall dividing us from God, and from one another, might be torn down for good. As we eat, remind us that we come to this table not as strangers seated at a distance, but as members of one household, welcomed in by Your own broken body. Search us, unite us, and make us one, even as we are one with You. In Jesus' name, Amen.

**Prayer for the Cup**

Heavenly Father, we lift this cup in remembrance of the blood of the new covenant, poured out that those who were once far off might be brought near. Seal in us today the peace this blood has purchased — peace with You, and peace with the brothers and sisters seated beside us. Keep us faithful until we share this cup again with Christ in Your coming kingdom. In Jesus' name, Amen.
