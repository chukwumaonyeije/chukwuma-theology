# Invocation and Offering TTS Narrative — July 11, 2026
### Communion Sabbath — Ephesians 2:14–19

[PAUSE — 2 seconds]

Gracious and Reconciling Father,

We come before You this Communion Sabbath with hearts humbled by the memory of what it cost to bring us near. We gather not as strangers who happen to occupy the same pew, but as a people You have made one — bought back, brought near, and built together into a dwelling place for Your Spirit.

Your Word reminds us this morning, "For he himself is our peace, who has made the two groups one and has destroyed the barrier, the dividing wall of hostility... Consequently, you are no longer foreigners and strangers, but fellow citizens with God's people and also members of his household." Lord, we confess how easily we rebuild the walls Christ already tore down — walls of pride, of distance, of judgment toward one another. Tear them down again today.

As we prepare to come to Your table, search us. Let nothing remain between us and You, and nothing remain between us and one another, that has not first been laid at the foot of the cross. Fill this sanctuary with the peace that Christ Himself preached — to those who were far off, and to those who were near.

We lift up our elders and leaders as they prepare to serve this table. Steady their hands and soften our hearts, that this Communion service would be more than ritual — that it would be the real and present reminder of the body broken and the blood poured out for us.

Gracious Lord, we thank You for the privilege of this Sabbath rest, and for the even greater privilege of being called, in Christ, members of one household. May everything we do this morning — our singing, our giving, our remembering — flow from that one peace You purchased for us.

In the name of Jesus Christ, our peace and our Redeemer, we pray. Amen.

[PAUSE — 3 seconds]

It is time for our Tithes and Offering.

On November 9th, 1989, a wall that had stood for twenty-eight years came down. For nearly three decades, the Berlin Wall did more than divide a city — it divided families, congregations, and generations of the same people from each other, sealed off by concrete, barbed wire, and searchlights. People who once worshiped in the same buildings became strangers to one another, separated by a wall they did not build and could not tear down themselves. And then, in a single night, it fell — and people who had not spoken to family members in twenty-eight years walked across the rubble into each other's arms.

That is the image the apostle Paul reaches for in our sermon text this morning. He writes: "For he himself is our peace, who has made the two groups one and has destroyed the barrier, the dividing wall of hostility... His purpose was to create in himself one new humanity out of the two, thus making peace." Long before Berlin, the deepest wall any of us ever lived behind was the one between sinful humanity and a holy God — and no summit, no treaty, no human effort ever brought that one down. Only the cross did that.

That is what makes this Communion Sabbath, and this offering, different from an ordinary appeal for funds. When we give today, we are not simply funding a budget line. We are participating, in a small and grateful way, in the same movement of Christ — who "gave up his divine privileges... and humbled himself" to bring down the wall between us and God, and between us and each other. Every dollar we return is a small echo of a much larger gift already given.

There are several ways for you to give today. You may choose to contribute through Adventist Giving on the Atlanta North website, by scanning the QR code, or by texting "GIVE" to 770-818-4323. You can also use the SDA Giving App on your Android or iPhone, or place your gift in the offering box located in the foyer.

Let us pray for our tithes and offerings.

Heavenly Father, we thank You that in Christ the dividing wall has come down — between us and You, and between us and one another. As we bring our tithes and offerings this Communion Sabbath, let them be more than currency; let them be an act of worship from people who know how much peace cost. Bless what is given, and bless the hands that give it. Use these offerings to carry the message of reconciliation to those who still live behind walls of their own. In Jesus' name, we pray. Amen.

Thank you for your generous hearts. God bless you all.

[PAUSE — 3 seconds]

Let us now pray for the bread.

Gracious Lord, as we come together as one body — no longer strangers, but fellow citizens and members of Your household — we break this bread in remembrance of the body of Christ, broken that the wall between us might be destroyed. Let this bread remind us that in His flesh He made us one. Strengthen our unity as we partake, and let no division remain among us that His broken body has not already healed. In the name of Jesus Christ, Amen.

[PAUSE — 2 seconds]

Let us pray for the cup.

Heavenly Father, we lift this cup in remembrance of the blood of the new covenant, poured out that we, who were once far off, might be brought near. As we partake of this cup, let it seal again the peace Christ purchased for us — peace with You, and peace with one another. May we leave this table as one new humanity, reconciled and renewed. In Jesus' name, Amen.
