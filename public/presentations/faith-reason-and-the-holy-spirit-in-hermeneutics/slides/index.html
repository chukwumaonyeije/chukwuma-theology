Foundation

The Core Conviction

The entire Bible is the propositional, infallible Word of God. What the text meant originally is, in principle, what it means today. Proper hermeneutics requires the integration of three essential elements:

Sanctified Reason

Human reason is central — but it must be renewed and transformed by the Spirit to become a trustworthy interpretive tool.

Living Faith

Faith and reason are complementary. When they conflict, Scripture takes priority over every human claim or system.

The Holy Spirit

The Spirit who inspired the Bible is the same Spirit who must illuminate it. He is not helpful — He is indispensable.

Part 1  ·  Faith and Reason

The Problem: Sin Has Damaged Human Reason

Hermeneutics is a rational process — but the biblical worldview holds that the Fall has impacted human rationality at its core. Unregenerate reason, left to itself, is structurally incapable of correctly interpreting the Word of God.

Jeremiah 17:9
"The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked; who can know it?"
1 Corinthians 2:14
"The natural man receiveth not the things of the Spirit of God: for they are foolishness unto him; neither can he know them, because they are spiritually discerned."
Part 1  ·  Faith and Reason

The Solution: Sanctified Reason

Romans 12:2
"Be transformed by the renewing of your mind, that you may prove what is that good and acceptable and perfect will of God."
2 Corinthians 10:5
"Bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ."
What Sanctified Reason Looks Like
  • Still thinks carefully and evaluates evidence
  • Operates with humility and spiritual openness
  • Submits to Scripture when conflicts arise
  • Empowered by the Spirit (Titus 3:5)
  • Refined by the grace of Christ (Ellen White)
Part 2  ·  The Cosmic Context

The Cosmic Conflict and Hermeneutics

We do not study the Bible in a neutral environment. Supernatural beings actively seek to shape how human beings read Scripture.

Holy Angels Assist
  • Gabriel sent to give Daniel understanding (Dan 8:16)
  • Angels illuminated the resurrection (Luke 24:6-7)
  • Assisted Luther and William Miller (Ellen White)
  • Humble, prayerful study invites illumination
Fallen Angels Corrupt
  • Deceiving spirits and doctrines of demons (1 Tim 4:1)
  • Satan blinds the minds of unbelievers (2 Cor 4:3-4)
  • Word opened without reverence invites error (GC 704-705)
  • Our posture determines our assistant
"The spirit in which you come to the investigation of the Scriptures will determine the character of the assistant at your side."
Ellen White  ·  Testimonies to Ministers, 108
Part 3  ·  The Holy Spirit

The Indispensable Role of the Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit is not merely helpful in biblical interpretation. He is indispensable. Three dimensions of His work:

He Guides

The Spirit of truth guides believers into all truth (John 16:13). This guidance is communal — guarding against isolated, charismatic interpretation.

He Transforms

The Spirit equips the interpreter with the mind of Christ (Phil 2:5). The carnal mind is enmity with God — the mind of Christ is its antidote.

He Illuminates

The Spirit brings to remembrance all that Jesus said (John 14:26), directing the comparison of scripture with scripture and rewarding the search.

"It is the Holy Spirit's office to direct this search and to reward it."
Ellen White  ·  1888 Materials, 4:1538
Summary

Baldwin's Four-Point Framework

The Spirit's role in hermeneutics, summarized:

  • 1 Guidance: The Holy Spirit guides the hermeneutical process from start to finish.
  • 2 Transformation: Spirit baptism equips the interpreter with the mind of Christ — a softened, unveiled, and responsive mind.
  • 3 Recall: The Spirit brings biblical truths and images to the mind of the interpreter during study.
  • 4 Illumination: The Spirit illuminates the interpreter's mind with fresh, living meaning from the text.
"Without the supernatural assistance of the Holy Spirit and heavenly angels, there can be no proper interpretation of the truths of Scripture, no matter how hard one engages the rational powers."
John T. Baldwin

Elder Discussion

Reflect, Discuss, Apply

  • Q1 How does the distinction between unregenerate and sanctified reason shape how we approach our own Bible study and how we lead Sabbath School discussions?
  • Q2 Our posture determines our assistant. How practically can elders cultivate and model the right posture in their own study and teaching?
  • Q3 When a member brings an interpretation conflicting with Adventist doctrine, what does applying faith over reason look like pastorally — not authoritarianly?
  • Q4 What does having the mind of Christ (Phil 2:5) look like practically in sermon or lesson preparation? How is it different from careful intellectual preparation alone?
  • Q5 The Spirit guides a community, not isolated individuals. How does our eldership team function as a communal hermeneutical check on each other?
