<div class="eyebrow">Atlanta North Seventh-day Adventist Church</div>

# Adults Try, Children Trust

<div class="subtitle">Finding real peace by overcoming adulthood</div>

<div class="accent-line"></div>

<p class="reference">Key Scripture: Luke 18:15-17</p>

<p class="credit">First sermon preached by Pastor Evan Knott at Atlanta North Seventh-day Adventist Church - May 16, 2026</p>

---

<!-- _class: split -->

<div>
  <div class="eyebrow">The opening story</div>
  <h2>The pressure of transition</h2>
  <div class="accent-line"></div>
</div>

<div class="panel">
  <ul>
    <li>The anxiety of moving and transition.</li>
    <li>The mindset: <strong>"Just wait till Georgia"</strong> for peace.</li>
    <li>The realization: God wants us to experience peace <strong>now</strong>, not just survive the season.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

---

<div class="eyebrow">The illusion of the next season</div>

## We keep postponing peace

<div class="accent-line"></div>

<p class="wide">Adults often tell themselves that peace is waiting on the other side of a stressful event.</p>

<div class="grid two">
  <div class="card">
    <h3>What we say</h3>
    <ul class="small-list">
      <li>Just wait till the school year ends.</li>
      <li>Just wait till exams are finished.</li>
      <li>Just wait till I get that promotion.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="card alt">
    <h3>What it does</h3>
    <p>It turns peace into a destination instead of a gift received from God in the present tense.</p>
  </div>
</div>

---

<!-- _class: light -->

<div class="eyebrow">Normalizing busyness</div>

# Busy is not the same as faithful

<div class="accent-line"></div>

<ul>
  <li>We accept the tyranny of modern life.</li>
  <li>We put off the real peace God desires.</li>
  <li>We start believing exhaustion is the price of adulthood.</li>
</ul>

---

<!-- _class: quote -->

<div class="eyebrow">The lie of doing enough</div>

<div class="statement">We fall for the trap that we are only valuable if we accomplish our goals and earn our peace.</div>

<p class="dim">But grace does not wait for productivity to become true.</p>

---

<!-- _class: split -->

<div>
  <div class="eyebrow">Luke 18:15-16</div>
  <h2>Jesus values the helpless</h2>
  <div class="accent-line"></div>
</div>

<div class="panel">
  <ul>
    <li>In a society that devalued infants, Jesus stopped everything to welcome them.</li>
    <li>The disciples saw interruption. Jesus saw the kingdom.</li>
    <li>You do not need to be accomplished to come to Him.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

---

<!-- _class: split -->

<div>
  <div class="eyebrow">Luke 18:17</div>
  <h2>The kingdom belongs to the childlike</h2>
  <div class="accent-line"></div>
</div>

<div class="panel soft">
  <ul>
    <li>Jesus rebukes <em>faulty grown-up logic</em>, which says we must earn our way.</li>
    <li>We must receive the kingdom like a little child.</li>
    <li>The way in is simple trusting, not spiritual resume-building.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

---

<div class="eyebrow">The contrast</div>

## Trying versus trusting

<div class="accent-line"></div>

<div class="grid two">
  <div class="card">
    <h3>Luke 18:9-14</h3>
    <p>The Pharisee tried to earn favor through accomplishments.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="card alt">
    <h3>The tax collector</h3>
    <p>He did not negotiate with God. He simply asked for mercy.</p>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="eyebrow">The contrast continues</div>

## The rich ruler walks away sad

<div class="accent-line"></div>

<ul>
  <li>Luke 18:18-27 places another successful adult before Jesus.</li>
  <li>The rich ruler tried to earn eternal life but walked away sad.</li>
  <li>Jesus reminds us that salvation is impossible for mortals, but possible for God.</li>
</ul>

---

<!-- _class: quote -->

<div class="eyebrow">Sermon burden</div>

<div class="statement">Adults try. Children trust.</div>

<p>We must stop trying to earn peace and salvation through our own strength.</p>

<p>Instead, we rely entirely on God.</p>

---

<!-- _class: quote -->

<div class="eyebrow">Practical application</div>

<div class="application">Ask God what trusting Him looks like today in your current season.</div>

---

<div class="eyebrow">Practical application</div>

## Trust has a calendar

<div class="accent-line"></div>

<ul>
  <li>Set aside intentional time for your family or health.</li>
  <li>Trust God to make up the difference in your schedule.</li>
  <li>Let obedience interrupt the panic of proving yourself.</li>
</ul>

---

<!-- _class: light -->

<div class="eyebrow">Start small</div>

# Small trust is still trust

<div class="accent-line"></div>

<ul>
  <li>Spend 15 minutes with God.</li>
  <li>Read a Gospel.</li>
  <li>Be honest about your fears and excitements.</li>
</ul>

---

<!-- _class: split -->

<div>
  <div class="eyebrow">The resolution</div>
  <h2>The adopted little girl</h2>
  <div class="accent-line"></div>
</div>

<div class="panel">
  <ul>
    <li>She tried to disqualify herself from Disney World because she thought she was not good enough.</li>
    <li>Her father assured her she was going because she was part of the family.</li>
    <li>The point is not performance. The point is belonging.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

---

<!-- _class: quote -->

<div class="eyebrow">The father's word</div>

<div class="statement">"I finally got to go to Disney World. But it was not because I was good. It was because I am yours."</div>

---

<!-- _class: quote -->

<div class="eyebrow">Closing summary</div>

<div class="closing-summary">We do not get to heaven by trying harder. We get there by being His.</div>

<p class="dim">You do not have to earn it. You never actually did.</p>

---

<!-- _class: quote -->

<div class="eyebrow">Invitation</div>

<div class="statement">Jesus is asking, "Are you part of this family?"</div>

<p>If you say yes, He says, "And you are coming with me."</p>

<p>Choose today to be God's child and experience freedom from trying harder.</p>

---

<!-- _class: blessing -->

<div class="eyebrow">Final blessing</div>

<div class="statement">May you experience the peace of a child who knows exactly whose they are. Amen.</div>
