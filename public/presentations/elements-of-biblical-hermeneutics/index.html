Chapter Overview

  • No interpreter reads with a blank mind — presuppositions shape everything
  • The Bible's nature demands a method calibrated to its unique divine-human character
  • A faithful hermeneutic flows from Scripture's own self-testimony

Central question: Are our interpretive presuppositions accountable to Scripture — or to the spirit of the age?

Key Concept

The Object Determines the Method

You cannot measure a sprint with a yardstick. You cannot analyze a poem by testing the ink.

The Bible — divinely breathed, humanly expressed — demands a method open to supernatural reality.

  • Methods that assume God does not exist are ill-suited to their object
  • Adventist hermeneutics refuses etsi Deus non daretur — "as if God did not exist"

Key Concept

The Christological Analogy

"The union of the divine and the human, manifest in Christ, exists also in the Bible." — Ellen G. White, Testimonies vol. 5
  • Scripture is truly God's Word AND truly human language
  • Human context is real — it must be taken seriously
  • The divine authority is not canceled by human form
4 / 15

How Did Jesus View Scripture?

  • "It is written" — Jesus's response to every temptation (Matt. 4:4, 7, 10)
  • "Scripture cannot be broken" (John 10:35)
  • "If you do not believe Moses, how will you believe me?" (John 5:46–47)

What Scripture says, God says. — John Stott

5 / 15

Scripture's Own Self-Testimony

Old Testament

  • God commanded writing: Moses, Jeremiah, Habakkuk
  • "The word of the LORD came to me" — foundational prophetic formula
  • Written word preserves, multiplies, and stabilizes revelation

New Testament

  • 2 Tim. 3:16 — all Scripture God-breathed
  • 2 Pet. 1:21 — not human will, but Holy Spirit
  • NT writings cited on par with OT (1 Tim. 5:18; 2 Pet. 3:16)
6 / 15

The Historical-Grammatical Method

  • Prose is read as prose; poetry as poetry; parable as parable
  • Meaning is determined by the text itself — not an outside key
  • Hebrew, Aramaic, Greek grammar and context illuminate the text
  • Stands against both allegorism and naturalistic historical criticism

Luther: "The Holy Spirit uses no more than the plain meaning of words."

7 / 15

Scripture Interprets Scripture (Tota Scriptura)

"To the law and to the testimony! If they do not speak according to this word, it is because they have no dawn." — Isaiah 8:20
  • The canon is the primary interpretive resource
  • New revelation is tested by established revelation
  • Difficult texts illuminated by clearer texts
  • Unity of Scripture flows from the unity of its divine Author
8 / 15

Ellen G. White & the Adventist Distinctive

  • The Great Controversy frames hermeneutics: Satan first attacked the plain sense of God's Word
  • Scriptural skepticism is a slippery slope — Selected Messages 1:17–18
  • Holy Spirit as aid to Bible study — never to supersede it
  • EGW models a Scripture-shaped hermeneutic, not a magisterium that supplements it

"The Spirit was not given to supersede the Bible; the Word of God is the standard by which all teaching must be tested." — EGW, The Great Controversy

9 / 15

Hermeneutical Principle in Action

Text: Genesis 2:1–3 (the Sabbath)

  • Historical-grammatical: Who wrote it? What did "rest" mean in Hebrew narrative?
  • Canonical: How does Exodus 20:8–11, Isaiah 58:13, and Mark 2:27 illuminate this?
  • Tota Scriptura: The Sabbath's meaning grows, but does not contradict, across the canon
  • Obedient reading: Understanding is not complete until it shapes Sabbath practice

Five Biblical Presuppositions a Faithful Method Must Affirm

  • God exists — assume it; do not argue it into every text
  • God is supernatural — the method must be open to the transcendent
  • God acts in history — predictive prophecy and miracles are real
  • God is personal — He communicates propositionally in human language
  • Sin distorts thinking — the Holy Spirit's illumination is essential
13 / 15

Key Takeaway

"A hermeneutic that is faithful to Scripture is not a method imposed on the Bible from outside — it is a posture grown from within Scripture itself, shaped by what Jesus taught, what the apostles practiced, and what the text explicitly claims about its own origin and authority."

The method (historical-grammatical, canonical, Spirit-illumined, obedient) must answer to the object (the divinely-breathed, humanly-expressed Word of God).
