Opening — The Patient Who Felt Fine

She Felt Completely Well.

A mother expecting a child. No pain. No bleeding. No warning of any kind.
By everything she could see and feel — all was well.

But we do not go only by what she feels.
We measure. We look beneath the surface.

She was not in pain. She was in danger.
The Spiritual Parallel

The Other Kind of Life

A Christian can pray. Can serve. Can give. Can teach. Can lead.
Can carry the whole outward shape of a faithful life —

— and be quietly losing the heart the entire time.

That is not a hypothetical. That is a man in the Bible.

Movement One — Two Verses, One Autopsy

The First Scan — AD 60–62

"Luke the beloved physician, and Demas, greet you."
— Colossians 4:14, NKJV

Demas is standing next to Luke.
Inside Paul's inner circle. Named in Scripture. Active. Present.

Everything looks normal.
Movement One — Two Verses, One Autopsy

The Second Scan — AD 67

"Demas has forsaken me,
having loved this present world."
— 2 Timothy 4:10, NKJV

No scandal. No public rebellion. No headline.

The cause of death was misdirected love.
Movement Two — He Did Not Fall. He Drifted.

The Crisis Is Never the Beginning

In medicine, the dramatic event is almost never the beginning of the disease.
It is the end of it. The crisis is when the body finally announces
what has been developing silently, in the dark, for a long time.

He did not fall. He drifted.
The Diagnosis — A Conflict of Affections

Word Study: Agapēsas

agapēsas (ἀγαπήσας)

The love word — the same word used for the love of God Himself. Demas gave his deepest affection and allegiance to the present age.

aiōn (αἰών)

Not the planet, not people — the present age. The spirit of the times. The mood of the moment. The rewards available now.

The great conflict is not a conflict of ideas.
It is a conflict of affections.
Story One — When the Outside Does Not Tell the Truth

Stable Is Not the Same as Well

She felt fine. She was not fine.
Pressure rising where it must not rise. A silent process, already advanced.
The window to act safely was closing — and she had felt almost none of it.

Feeling fine is not a diagnosis. It is a feeling.

The most dangerous diseases of the soul are the ones that let you feel fine
right up until the day they don't.

The Defining Difference

Proximity Is Not Surrender

You can stand close to the fire your whole life and never let it warm you.
You can be near the work without being given to the Worker.

Demas
had proximity
→
Luke
had surrender
Demas climbed. Luke knelt.
The Question for Every Competent Person

A Ladder — or an Offering?

Doctors. Nurses. Engineers. Business owners. Students.
People who left home and crossed oceans because they were good at something.

The question is not whether you have gifts.
The question is what your gifts are for.

A Ladder

Used for advancement. A monument to self. Climbed for the present age.

An Offering

Laid on the altar. Bent toward the kingdom. Given to the Worker.

Story Two — What Has Held Me Steady

Every Identity Whispers the Same Thing

The immigrant. The student. The doctor. The builder.
The husband. The father. The elder.

Each layer brought the same whisper:

"This is what matters now. Love this. Build this. Become this."

And it does not feel like drifting. It feels like winning.

The Only Thing That Has Held Me Steady

The One Who Does Not Change

It was never the achievement — the achievement is a moving target.
It was never the security — the security can be gone by Friday.
It was never the title — titles are borrowed.

Everything in my life has been temporary except Him.

That is what Demas let go of, and the thing Luke held onto — all the way to the end.

The Gospel Underneath All of This

The Physician Does Not Only Diagnose

Gold Refined in Fire

Tested, proven faith — forged through honest examination.

White Garments

His righteousness, to cover what we cannot fix ourselves.

Eye-Salve

Honest sight — to see ourselves at last, and see Him clearly.

He is not standing over the chart shaking His head.
He is holding the cure.
The Close — Check Your Vitals

Before You Leave This Room

Ask the Holy Spirit the hard question, and be quiet long enough to hear the answer.

What do I love most?

What occupies the center of my imagination when no one is watching?

What would I struggle to surrender?

If Christ asked me for it tonight — what would I hesitate to give?

Check your vitals.
The Physician is in. And the cure is His to give.
