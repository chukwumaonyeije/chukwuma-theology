

This sermon outline builds on your previous reflections regarding spiritual vigilance and the contrast between the "gradual decline" of Demas and the steadfastness of Luke. It is designed to be a 30-minute address, weaving together the rigor of your medical perspective with the warmth of your ministry.

## Sermon Title: The Fellowship of the Finish: Choosing the Path of Luke in a Demas World

---

### **I. Introduction: The Tragedy of the "Almost" Faithful**
* **The Hook:** Start with the roll call of Paul’s "fellow laborers" in Philemon 1:24. Mention that Demas and Luke started in the same room, with the same mission, and the same mentor.
* **The Problem:** The haunting words of 2 Timothy 4:10: *"For Demas hath forsaken me, having loved this present world."*
* **The Thesis:** Spiritual desertion is rarely a sudden cliff; it is a gradual erosion of "first love." We must choose daily whether we will be remembered for our departure or our endurance.

### **II. The Anatomy of Decline: Understanding Demas**
* **The Common Start:** Demas was once a "fellow laborer" (Philemon 1:24). He wasn't an outsider; he was an insider who lost his way.
* **The Diagnosis of the Heart:** Use the "gradual spiritual decline" theme. In your previous work, you’ve noted that professional and spiritual vigilance are linked. Demas didn't stop believing; he just started "loving this present world."
* **Biblical Verse:** **1 John 2:15** — *"Love not the world, neither the things that are in the world."*
* **E.G. White Insight:**
    > "Demas, who for a time had been a fellow laborer with the apostle, afterward forsook the cause of Christ... he chose the things of this life. How many today are doing the same thing!" (*The Acts of the Apostles*, p. 455)

### **III. The Physician’s Resolve: The Contrast of Luke**
* **The Silent Hero:** Contrast Demas’s exit with the short, powerful sentence in 2 Timothy 4:11: *"Only Luke is with me."* * **The "Beloved Physician" (Colossians 4:14):** Highlight Luke’s dual role. Like you, Luke was a professional who used his skills for the Kingdom. He didn’t let his "good work" in medicine distract him from his "first love" for the Master.
* **Cultural Connection:** For the Brasleton and Diaspora community, speak to the resilience required to remain faithful in a culture that rewards worldly success over spiritual depth. Luke chose the "cold prison" with Paul over the "comforts of Thessalonica."

### **IV. Reclaiming Your "First Love"**
* **The Admonition:** **Revelation 2:4-5** — *"Nevertheless I have somewhat against thee, because thou hast left thy first love."*
* **Spiritual Vigilance:** Address the young adults directly. The "present world" today is not just Thessalonica; it’s the digital noise, the career ladder, and the subtle shift from "serving God" to "serving self."
* **The "Good Work":** Connect this to **Philippians 1:6**. God began a "good work" in both Demas and Luke. The difference was that Luke allowed God to *perform* it until the day of Jesus Christ.

### **V. Conclusion: The Call to Finish**
* **The Final Contrast:** * **Demas:** Remembered for where he *went* (Thessalonica).
    * **Luke:** Remembered for where he *stayed* (At the side of the Gospel).
* **Call to Action:** Ask the congregation: "If Paul were writing his final letter today, where would your name be? Under the list of those who stayed, or those who sought the world?"
* **Closing Quote:** > "The greatest want of the world is the want of men—men who will not be bought or sold... men who are as true to duty as the needle to the pole." (*Education*, p. 57)
