# How the Sermon Producer Skill Works — TTS Narrative
### Speechify Training Briefing

[PAUSE — 2 seconds]

Welcome to the briefing on the Sermon Producer Skill. This guide is designed to explain how the sermon prep and production pipeline functions, helping you internalize the workflow while listening on Speechify.

[PAUSE — 1 second]

The core purpose of the Sermon Producer Skill is to systematically guide you through a structured, multi-phase process to construct deep, biblically grounded, and pastorally sensitive sermons. Every sermon is designed to speak in the distinctive Chukwuma Theology voice, which sits at the intersection of a physician’s explanatory clarity, a theologian’s analytical rigor, and an Adventist’s prophetic perspective.

To organize this work, the project repository uses a dedicated directory called Sermon Producer Files. Within this directory, each sermon is given a self-contained folder. For example, the Elijah sermon suite contains folders for workflow frameworks, core exegesis, a pastoral bridge, and existing drafts or slides. 

The workflow frameworks folder contains the research process and potential sermon frames. The core exegesis folder houses verse-by-verse interpretations, Hebrew word studies, and Ellen White integrations. The pastoral bridge folder provides language for connecting biblical tensions to modern realities, such as burnout, depression, and clinical boundaries. Finally, the drafts and slides folder contains the raw manuscripts and presentation materials.

[PAUSE — 2 seconds]

The sermon development process is divided into five sequential phases.

Phase one is Immersion. In this phase, the rule is to sit with the biblical text before you sit with the notes. You read the primary scripture slowly in at least three translations—such as the New King James Version, the English Standard Version, and the New Living Translation. You read the passage aloud, listen to it, and read key canonical cross-references. You then write short answers to diagnostic questions to feel the movement of the text before trying to explain it to anyone else.

Phase two is Theological Excavation. Here, you read the exegesis documents and highlight key themes. The focus is to look for what the text reveals about the character of God, the vulnerability of the faithful, the Great Controversy, the remnant, and how God speaks. You then distill a one-page list of the strongest theological claims and write a single-sentence controlling burden. Every paragraph in the sermon must serve this one controlling sentence.

Phase three is Sermon Architecture. You structure the message into a four-movement narrative arc that matches the text itself. In the Elijah sermon, for instance, the movements transition from Carmel to collapse, to the God who feeds before He explains, to the cave and the still small voice, and finally to the God who sends the exhausted prophet back into history. You also decide on the primary sermonic emphasis—whether it focuses on divine care, God's quiet presence, or a restored commission.

Phase four is Drafting. A key practice here is to draft the body of the sermon first, and write the introduction and conclusion last. This ensures the introduction serves the actual sermon rather than a generic concept. You integrate Adventist theology as load-bearing architecture, not decoration, letting scripture lead and using Ellen White’s writings to deepen the exposition. You also use the pastoral bridge materials to speak responsibly about human struggle without letting therapeutic categories overwhelm the biblical text.

Phase five is Refinement and automated pulpit formatting.

[PAUSE — 2 seconds]

A vital component of the Sermon Producer skill is the automation compiler, represented by scripts such as build speaker version. This script automatically compiles raw markdown sermon drafts into professional Word documents for delivery. 

As it compiles, the script performs three tasks. First, it inserts slide transition tags at exact paragraphs. Second, it places bold, colored pulpit delivery directions inline, advising the speaker when to pause for effect, when to slow down and emphasize, or when to speak with a warm, conversational tone. Third, it formats the document into a readable layout with custom fonts, margins, and highlight boxes. This ensures the manuscript is easy to navigate while standing at the pulpit.

By following this five-step sequence, the Sermon Producer skill ensures that your messages do not collapse into simple moralizing or clinical lectures. Instead, they remain focused on the character of God, leading the congregation through a deep, structured, and transformative worship experience.
