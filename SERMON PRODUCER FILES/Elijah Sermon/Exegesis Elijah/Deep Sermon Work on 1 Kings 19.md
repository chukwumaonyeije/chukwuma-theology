## Deep Sermon Work on 1 Kings 19
### An AI-Accelerated Study Framework

The Hassabis insight is this: AI doesn't replace deep thinking — it **compresses the ramp** so you get to the original, hard intellectual work faster. The goal below is exactly that. AI handles retrieval and scaffolding; you handle revelation and proclamation.

---

## The Six-Stage Framework

---

### Stage 1 — Immersion (You do this alone, no AI)
**Time: 30–45 min | Do this first**

Read 1 Kings 19 in three translations — NKJV, ESV, and a fresh modern one like NLT or The Message. Then read it aloud once. No notes. Just listening.

Write one sentence answering: *What does this text do to me?*

That sentence becomes your emotional north star for the whole sermon.

---

### Stage 2 — Exegetical Excavation (AI-accelerated)
**Time: 45–60 min | Use me as your study partner**

Come back and we work through these together, one at a time:

- **Hebrew word studies**: `רתם` (broom tree), `דרכו` (his journey), `יסף` (it is enough) — each one opens a different door
- **Narrative structure**: What is the chiastic pattern in chapter 19? How does the angel's touch in vv. 5–7 mirror the fire of Carmel in chapter 18?
- **Intertextual threads**: Moses at Horeb (Exodus 33), Jonah under the vine, the Psalms of Ascent — where does Elijah's collapse echo across the canon?
- **The still small voice (v.12)**: `קוֹל דְּמָמָה דַקָּה` — "a sound of thin silence." This is your sermon's hinge. Spend real time here.

**How to use me**: Paste the verse, ask the question. Go slow. Push back on what I give you. Ask *why* and *so what* repeatedly.

---

### Stage 3 — Clinical-Theological Bridge (AI + your MFM instincts)
**Time: 30 min**

This is where your identity as a physician-theologian becomes the sermon's distinctive power. Most preachers do one side. You can hold both.

Prompt me to help you work through:

- What does modern depression neuroscience actually say about withdrawal, anhedonia, and the desire to die — and how precisely does v.4 ("It is enough, LORD, take away my life") map onto clinical presentation?
- What is the **theological meaning of God not rebuking Elijah** but instead feeding him? What does that say about how heaven views mental illness?
- The Adventist distinctive: Ellen White on Elijah (*Prophets and Kings*, ch. 15 — "From Jezreel to Horeb") is extraordinary here. She names exhaustion, discouragement, and physical depletion explicitly. We use that.
- How does the investigative judgment framework relate to Elijah's self-assessment vs. God's assessment of him? The gap between how Elijah sees himself ("I alone am left") and the 7,000 God already knows about is theologically loaded.

---

### Stage 4 — Sermon Architecture (AI-structured, your voice)
**Time: 30 min**

Once we have Stage 2 and 3 done, I help you build a 45-minute structure. For Braselton SDA, I'd propose something like:

| Block | Content | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Opening** | A clinical moment — what depression actually feels like from the inside | 4 min |
| **Narrative anchor** | Read/retell 1 Kings 19:1–8 dramatically | 6 min |
| **Movement 1** | Elijah's collapse — naming it honestly | 8 min |
| **Movement 2** | God's response: food, sleep, touch — not theology first | 8 min |
| **Movement 3** | The cave, the question, and the thin silence | 10 min |
| **Movement 4** | "Go, return" — not healed, but commissioned | 5 min |
| **Landing** | The 7,000 you don't know about. You are not alone. | 4 min |

We adjust this together once the exegetical work is done.

---

### Stage 5 — Language Crafting (AI as writing partner)
**Time: 30–45 min**

Bring me your rough movement outlines and we work on:
- **Opening line** — this must earn the congregation's trust in the first 30 seconds on mental health
- **Transitional sentences** between movements
- **The landing** — the single image or sentence they carry out of the building
- Calibrating the clinical language for a Sabbath morning congregation — honest but not clinical, tender but not therapeutic

Your DWC voice rules apply here: short declarative sentences, no filler, first-person authority.

---

### Stage 6 — Stress-Testing (AI as critic)
**Time: 20 min**

Before you finalize, bring me the full outline and I will:
- Play a skeptical congregant: "Is this too clinical for church?"
- Play a mental health advocate: "Did you handle this responsibly?"
- Play an Adventist theologian: "Is the Ellen White use accurate and fair?"
- Identify any places where the sermon *talks about* Elijah without ever landing on the people sitting in the room
