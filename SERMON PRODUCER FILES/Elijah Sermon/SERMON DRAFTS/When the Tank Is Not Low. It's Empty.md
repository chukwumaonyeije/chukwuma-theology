# When the Tank Is Not Low. It's Empty.
### What God Does When You Have Nothing Left

**Text:** 1 Kings 19:1-18
**Occasion:** Sabbath Morning Worship — Braselton Seventh-day Adventist Church
**Speaker:** Dr. Chukwuma Onyeije
**Estimated Time:** 38–45 minutes at a moderate pace

> **Central Proposition:** When you arrive at empty, God's first response is not correction or commission — it is care. And from that care, He restores and recommissions broken people.

---

## I. Introduction — The Boy From the Bronx Who Loved a Runner
*Personal narrative opening — 4–6 minutes*

Begin warmly, unhurried. Let the personal story breathe.

---

"I grew up in the Bronx. And when I was ten years old, my family moved
to Liverpool — upstate New York. Not England. Liverpool, New York.
And the church we joined was called **Mount Carmel Seventh-day Adventist Church**."

*Pause. Let the name land.*

"Mount Carmel. If you know your Bible, you know what happened on
Mount Carmel. And I can tell you — at that church, we heard about it.
Almost every quarter. Elijah and the prophets of Baal. Fire from
heaven. The chariot race. The rain cloud the size of a man's hand.
I heard that story so many times as a boy that I had it memorized
before I knew what memorized meant."

"But here is what I loved most about Elijah as a child. He was strong.
He was courageous. And he was **fast**. The Bible says he outran a chariot.
A chariot. With horses. I was ten years old and I thought — that is my
Bible hero."

"For years I said Elijah was my favorite. Then in college I discovered
Isaiah and I thought — okay, maybe Isaiah. But the more I come back
to the text, the more I think Elijah really is it. Not because he called
down fire. But because of what happens **the morning after**."

"See, as a child I loved chapter 18. It was not until college that I
actually sat down and studied chapter 19. And what I found there
changed the way I read the Bible — and honestly, changed the way I
understand God."

"Chapter 19 is not about fire. It is about **bread**. It is about **sleep**.
It is about a still small voice. And it is about a God who does not
abandon broken people. He feeds them."

*State the title and central proposition clearly before moving into the body.*

## Background — You Cannot Understand Chapter 19 Without Chapter 18
*1–2 minutes — contextual setup*

- Elijah's greatest day: stood alone against 450 prophets of Baal on Mount Carmel
- Called down fire. Executed judgment. Outran a chariot.
- By every measure — a triumph. The nation should have turned back to God.
- Then Jezebel sends one message.
- One sentence. From one woman. And the man who outran a chariot is now running the other direction.

> **"The distance between your greatest victory and your deepest collapse can be one bad message. One phone call. One email. One conversation after church."**

"This is not weakness. This is what happens when you pour out everything
you have for God — and the world responds as though none of it mattered."

## II. Point One — God Meets Us at Empty, Not After Empty
**Text: 1 Kings 19:3–7**
*10–12 minutes*

### A. The Prayer Under the Juniper Tree (v. 4)

*"It is enough, Lord. Take away my life. I am not better than my fathers."*

- Hebrew: ***rav*** — abundant, more than sufficient, past full measure.
  The same root used when God tells Abraham his reward will be "very great."
  Elijah is not saying *a little.* He is saying: this exceeds my capacity.
  I have reached the outer limit of what I am able to bear.
- This is not a crisis of faith — he is still talking to God.
- **This is a crisis of capacity.**
- His honesty is itself an act of faith.

### B. What God Does NOT Do

When Elijah bottoms out under the juniper tree, God does **not**:

- Rebuke him for running
- Remind him of his calling
- Replay the victories of Mount Carmel
- Assign him a new task
- Tell him to pray harder or trust more

Not one word of correction. Not one word of exhortation.

### C. What God DOES Do

- Sends an angel. With bread. And water.
- *"Arise and eat."* That is the entire message. (v. 5)
- Lets him sleep fully. Comes again a second time.
- Touches him **gently** — not urgently.
- *"The journey is too great for you."* (v. 7)
  God acknowledges the limitation. He does not pretend it does not exist. He meets it.

### Illustration — Clinical Parallel

> "In medicine, when a patient comes in broken down and depleted, we do not
> start with a lecture about what they should have done differently.
> We stabilize first. We meet the acute need. God is the best clinician
> in the room. He knows: you cannot give what you do not have.
> Stabilize first. Commission later."

### SDA Distinctive — Sabbath Connection

> "God rested on the seventh day and called it holy. He did not rest
> because He was tired. He rested to show us that rest is built into
> the architecture of creation. Sabbath is not a reward for finishing
> your work. It is a reminder that you were never meant to run without
> stopping. Elijah did not get to Sabbath rest. And it cost him.
> God gave it back to him — one meal and one sleep at a time."

### Landing Statement

> **"God's first response to your emptiness is not a new assignment. It is a meal. Let that change how you treat yourself — and how you treat each other."**

## III. Point Two — The God Who Speaks in the Silence
**Text: 1 Kings 19:9–13**
*10–12 minutes*

### A. The Question God Asks (v. 9)

*"What are you doing here, Elijah?"*

- Not a rebuke. A clinical intake question.
- God wants Elijah to say out loud what he has been carrying silently.
- **Lament requires language. Healing often begins when you name the thing.**
- Elijah's answer is raw and partially wrong — "I alone am left" —
  but God does not correct the theology yet. He just listens.

### B. The Three Events on the Mountain (vv. 11–12)

| Event | Power | God Present? |
|---|---|---|
| Wind that tears the mountains | Catastrophic | No |
| Earthquake | Undeniable | No |
| Fire | Impossible to ignore | No |
| ***qol demamah daqah*** | The sound of thin silence | **Yes** |

- ***Qol demamah daqah*** — three Hebrew words.
  KJV: "still small voice." But the Hebrew is more unsettling.
  It is almost the sound of no sound — a whisper so faint it exists
  at the edge of perception.

### C. Why Silence, Not Spectacle?

- Elijah has been living in the loud — Carmel, the chariot, the death threat,
  40 days in the desert. His soul has been running at threshold for months.
- God does not meet him with more loud.
  He meets him **on the other side of it.**

> **"God made noise to show Elijah He could. Then He chose silence to show Elijah who He was."**

### Illustration — Diagnostic Parallel

> "In medicine we learn early: the loudest finding is not always the most
> important one. A patient with a dramatic presentation may have a subtle
> finding on the ECG that tells you what is actually happening.
> Diagnosis requires stillness. You have to slow down enough to hear
> what the data is saying. God knew Elijah needed a diagnostic moment.
> Not more power. Presence."

### Great Controversy Connection

> "Ellen White writes that the great controversy is not primarily a war
> of force — it is a war of character. Satan uses spectacle. God uses
> truth. The still small voice is not weakness. It is the signature of
> a God who does not need to shout to be sovereign."

### Landing Statement

> **"What noise in your life right now is making it impossible to hear the thin silence? God is still speaking. But He may be speaking at a frequency that requires you to stop."**

---

## IV. Point Three — God Commissions Broken People
**Text: 1 Kings 19:13–18**
*8–10 minutes*

### A. God Repeats the Question (v. 13)

- Same words. Same tone. *"What are you doing here, Elijah?"*
- Elijah gives the same answer. He has not moved yet.
- He is not fixed. Not fully restored. Still convinced he is alone.

### B. God Gives Him an Assignment Anyway (vv. 15–17)

- Go back. Anoint Hazael. Anoint Jehu. Anoint Elisha.
- No waiting for Elijah to get it all together first.
- **Grace does not require full restoration before it trusts you again.**

### C. The Correction God Finally Gives — Gently (v. 18)

- *"I have reserved 7,000 in Israel who have not bowed to Baal."*
- Not a rebuke. A revelation.
- Elijah's loneliest belief — "I alone am left" — was factually wrong.
- **God corrects the theology only after He has met the need.
  He earns the right to correct by caring first.**

### Illustration — Personal

> "I have had moments in my career — as a physician, as an elder —
> where I was convinced I was the only one still trying. Still caring.
> Still showing up. And every single time, God eventually let me see
> the 7,000. The colleague who was quietly faithful. The member who
> was praying without announcement. You are never as alone as the
> enemy wants you to believe."

### SDA Distinctive — Sanctuary / Intercession

> "The sanctuary model reminds us that we have a High Priest who is
> actively interceding. Hebrews 4:15 — He was touched with the feeling
> of our infirmities. He knows the juniper tree. He has been to His
> own Gethsemane. And He is not watching from a distance.
> He is at the altar, presenting your name."

### Landing Statement

> **"God commissions broken people. That has always been His method. Not because brokenness is ideal — but because it is honest. And honest is exactly where God does His best work."**

---

## V. Conclusion and Closing Appeal
*5–7 minutes*

### Reframe the Personal Opening

"I told you about a ten-year-old boy at Mount Carmel SDA Church
who loved Elijah because he was strong and fast and called down fire.
What that boy did not yet understand — what took years of medicine,
and ministry, and life — is that Elijah's greatest moment is not
chapter 18. It is chapter 19.

Not the fire. **The bread.**
Not the chariot race. **The sleep.**
Not the 450 prophets. **The one angel.**
Not the mountain spectacle. **The thin silence.**"

### Direct Address to Congregation

"Someone in this room today is under a juniper tree. You may have
just come off your Mount Carmel — the hardest week, the biggest effort,
the thing you gave everything to. And the response was not what you
expected. And you are done. Not tired. **Done.**

God sees the empty tank.
He knows the journey was too great.
And He is not waiting here to correct you.
He is waiting here **with bread.**

This Sabbath morning is not an accident. God called you here — not
to give you another assignment. To feed you. To let you rest.
The Sabbath is God's weekly reminder that He still sends the angel.
He still bakes the bread. He still lets you sleep.

And when you are ready — not fully healed, not completely restored,
but ready — He will ask you: *what are you doing here?*
And He will trust you with something new."

### Closing Appeal

"If you are empty today — and you know who you are — I want to invite
you to receive this Sabbath as a gift, not an obligation. Let God
meet you where you are. Not where you think you should be.

If you are running from something — a threat, a failure, a season
that broke you — you do not have to keep running.
There is a juniper tree with your name on it.
And the angel is already on the way."

### Closing Scripture

*Read slowly. Let it settle.*

> *"And the angel of the Lord came again the second time, and touched him,
> and said, Arise and eat; because the journey is too great for thee."*
> — 1 Kings 19:7, KJV

*Close with prayer.*
