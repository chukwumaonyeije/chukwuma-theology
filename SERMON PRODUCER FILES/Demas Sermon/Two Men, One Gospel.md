## Sermon Outline: **"Two Men, One Gospel"**
*The Contrasting Trajectories of Demas and Luke*

**Setting:** Braselton Seventh-day Adventist Church | **Length:** 30 minutes
**Tone:** Pastoral, diagnostic, urgent — not condemning
**Central Scripture:** 2 Timothy 4:10–11

> *"Demas has forsaken me, having loved this present world, and has departed to Thessalonica — Crescens to Galatia, Titus to Dalmatia. Only Luke is with me."*

---

### The Thesis You'll Drive Toward

Both men stood in the same room, heard the same gospel, served the same apostle, and faced the same world. One stayed. One left. The difference was not talent, not knowledge, not even outward obedience. It was the location of their love.

---

## I. THE HOOK — The Last Dispatch (3 minutes)

Open with the scene. No setup. Drop the congregation directly into the text.

Paul is in a Roman prison. Second imprisonment. Not house arrest this time — a cold cell. Execution is close. He writes what will be his last letter. And in four verses at the end of that letter, he takes attendance.

> *Demas... has departed. Only Luke is with me.*

Two names. Two outcomes. Same mission field. Same pressures. Same world pulling at both of them.

**Staccato kicker:** *One man stayed. The other drifted. And the difference is the whole sermon.*

Tell the congregation: This is not a story about dramatic apostasy. No one in this room is about to worship idols or deny Christ in a stadium. This is a story about something quieter and more dangerous — the slow, unannounced migration of the heart from Christ to comfort.

---

## II. GROUNDING — The Three Mentions and the Full Trajectory (5 minutes)

Walk the biblical evidence with care. The arc is the argument.

**Demas:**
- **Colossians 4:14** — *"Demas greets you."* He is in Paul's inner circle. Named. Trusted. Working.
- **Philemon 1:24** — *"My fellow laborer."* Same standing. Still there.
- **2 Timothy 4:10** — *"Having loved this present world."* Gone.

The Greek is precise. *Agapēsas ton nun aiōna* — he gave to the world the love that belongs to God. Not admiration. Not occasional indulgence. Love. The word used in John 3:16. The word used in the Great Commandment. He applied that word to the present age.

**Luke:**
- **Colossians 4:14** — Named alongside Demas. *"Luke, the beloved physician."*
- **2 Timothy 4:11** — Same final letter. Same Roman prison. Same moment of pressure. *"Only Luke is with me."*

Luke shows up in the text when everyone else has departed or been sent away. Not because he had no options. Not because the world wasn't pulling at him. But because something held the center.

**Bridge:** Ellen White, in *The Acts of the Apostles*, describes Luke as one who subordinated every professional gift to the gospel mission — a physician who healed bodies but understood that the soul's need was greater. He was present not because he was stranded. He was present because he had settled the question of love.

---

## III. THE DIAGNOSTIC MIDDLE — What the Demas Condition Looks Like Now (8 minutes)

This is where the sermon becomes personal. Address the congregation directly.

Most people in this room are not Demas yet. But none of us are automatically Luke either. The trajectory — not the position — is what matters.

**Diagnosis 1: The drift from urgency to heritage**

Demas heard Paul preach the Second Coming. He witnessed miracles. He carried the urgent conviction that Christ was returning and the present world was passing away. Somewhere in that trajectory, *soon* became background music. The world stopped feeling temporary.

Ellen White writes in *The Great Controversy*: *"It is not the open, avowed enemies of the gospel that most endanger the church... but those who claim to be Christians and yet slowly retreat from their first love."* (Adapt as needed — quote the concept if not the exact passage you confirm.)

Ask the congregation: When did you last feel the urgency you felt the first time you understood what the gospel actually meant?

**Diagnosis 2: The substitution of good work for first love**

Here is what is subtle about Demas: he was not idle when he drifted. He was productive. He was building something. Thessalonica was not a vacation — it was a city with opportunity.

The Laodicean parallel is intentional. Paul wrote to the Colossians — the same letter where Demas appears healthy — and specifically instructed them to share that letter with Laodicea. A few decades later, Christ addresses Laodicea in Revelation 3: *"You say, I am rich... and have need of nothing. But you do not know that you are wretched, miserable, poor, blind, and naked."*

**Staccato kicker:** *The most dangerous spiritual condition is not rebellion. It is comfortable productivity with a cold heart.*

**Diagnosis 3: The love question**

Jesus asked Peter the same thing three times: *"Do you love Me?"* Not: Do you serve Me? Do you work for Me? Do you attend for Me? Love.

Ask the congregation: What would you struggle to surrender if Christ asked for it this week?

---

## IV. THE CONTRAST — What Luke Teaches Us (7 minutes)

Now pivot to Luke. This is where the sermon moves from diagnosis to hope.

Luke was a physician. In the first-century Roman world, that meant education, options, and mobility. He was exactly the kind of person who had every reason to find a comfortable arrangement — a patron, a practice, a way to do good work without the risk of being associated with a condemned prisoner.

He chose the prison cell.

Three things that explain Luke's fidelity:

**1. He wrote himself out of his own narrative.**
Read Luke and Acts carefully. He is present in the "we" passages — *"we sailed," "we stayed"* — but he never inserts his name. He was not building his own story. He was documenting Someone else's.

This is the disposition that holds. Not self-erasure. Self-subordination. The self placed in proper relationship to Christ.

**2. He practiced what he documented.**
Luke recorded the parable of the Prodigal Son — the father running to meet the returning child. He recorded the healing of the woman who had been bent over for eighteen years. He had the theological vocabulary of radical grace. And then he lived it — staying with a man who was going to die, because fidelity to that man was fidelity to Christ.

**3. He carried the "only."**
*"Only Luke is with me."*

That word — *only* — is not a footnote. It is Paul's pastoral testimony. One man's faithfulness became, in Paul's final hours, the embodiment of the entire body of Christ. You never know when your presence will be someone's *only*.

**Ellen White, *The Acts of the Apostles*:** Paraphrase her description of Luke's commitment — his medical skill as a means to the deeper mission, his constancy through opposition, his fidelity to Paul through multiple imprisonments.

---

## V. THE INTERIOR TURN — I Am Not Standing Outside This (4 minutes)

Do not skip this section. Give it real space.

Be honest with the congregation. You are a physician. You build things. You move fast. You teach Sabbath School, write, preach, carry multiple missions — and sometimes, in that volume, you can feel the pull.

Not toward obvious sin. Toward something quieter. The satisfaction of a productive week that had very little room for the presence of God. The theology remains correct. The schedule remains full. The heart has slowly moved.

That is the Demas condition. That is also my condition, if I'm not careful.

And here is what makes this room — any room — the right place to say this: we are all somewhere on the trajectory. The question is not whether we have drifted. The question is whether we are honest enough to stop and ask where we are standing right now, and what we love most.

---

## VI. THE CLOSE — The Prescription (3 minutes)

The Laodicean letter does not end with diagnosis. It ends with prescription.

> *"I counsel you to buy from Me gold refined in the fire... and anoint your eyes with eye salve, that you may see."* (Revelation 3:18)

The thing that Laodicea needed — and that we need — cannot be purchased with productivity. It cannot be earned with ministry. It has to be received. The eye salve of the Spirit, applied to vision that has grown comfortable. The gold that comes only through fire.

Ellen White's counsel on Laodicea, *Testimonies for the Church*, Vol. 4: describe her call for the church to receive the eyesalve — the Holy Spirit — that restores the capacity to see both Christ's beauty and our own need.

Close with the direct invitation:

The sermon is almost done. But the trajectory continues. You will leave this building and return to a world that is — in Paul's language — *this present age*. The same age Demas loved. The same age Luke navigated faithfully.

You will make choices this week that are small. Probably invisible to everyone around you. But they are, in aggregate, the answer to the question: Which man am I becoming?

**Final staccato kicker:**
*Luke stayed. So can you.*
*Not in your own strength. In the strength of the One who stayed for you.*
*Go love Him first.*
