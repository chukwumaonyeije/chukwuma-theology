# Sermon Notation: "Two Men, One Gospel"
## Demas, Luke, and the Question of First Love
**Braselton Seventh-day Adventist Church — Sabbath Morning Sermon**
**Preacher:** Chukwuma Onyeije, MD | **Target Length:** 30 minutes

---

## Core Thesis

Both men stood in the same room, heard the same gospel, served the same apostle, and faced the same world pulling at them. One drifted. One stayed. The difference was not talent, not theological knowledge, not even outward faithfulness. It was the location of their love. This sermon asks one question: *Where is yours?*

---

**2 Timothy 4:10–11** (KJV)

## Greek Word Studies

### 1. *ἀγαπήσας* — *agapēsas* (2 Timothy 4:10)
- **Form:** Aorist active participle of *agapaō*
- **Literal:** "having loved" — a completed action that explains his departure
- **Key:** This is the *same root word* as John 3:16 ("God so *loved* the world"), the Great Commandment ("thou shalt *love* the Lord thy God"), and John 21:15–17 (Jesus asking Peter "dost thou *love* me?")
- **Preaching move:** Paul does not say Demas feared the world, admired the world, or enjoyed the world. He *loved* it. He gave to the present age the affection that properly belongs to God.
- **Quote it directly:** "The word Paul uses is *agapēsas* — the word of John 3:16. The word of the Great Commandment. The word Christ asked Peter three times on the shore. He applied that word to the age that is passing away."

### 2. *αἰῶνα* — *aiōna* (2 Timothy 4:10)
- **Form:** Accusative of *aiōn*
- **Literal:** "this present age" — not merely geography or physical pleasure
- **Key:** *Aiōn* refers to the entire system of values, allegiances, and assumptions belonging to this era before Christ's return. It is the world as a rival ordering of reality.
- **Preaching move:** Demas did not necessarily become dissolute. He embraced the *aiōn* — the present world's way of measuring worth, security, and meaning.
- **Quote it directly:** "The Greek says *ton nun aiōna* — this present age. Not just pleasures. An entire rival system of meaning."

### 3. *μόνος* — *monos* (2 Timothy 4:11)
- **Form:** Nominative masculine singular adjective
- **Literal:** "only," "alone," "solitary"
- **Key:** "Only Luke is with me." The word lands like a stone. Not "chiefly" or "especially." *Only.*
- **Preaching move:** One person's faithfulness becomes, in Paul's darkest hour, the entire presence of the body of Christ. You never know when your staying will be someone's *only*.

## Preaching Mechanics

### The Staccato Kickers (deploy these at their marked moments)
1. *"One man stayed. The other drifted. And the difference is the whole sermon."*
2. *"The most dangerous spiritual condition is not rebellion. It is comfortable productivity with a cold heart."*
3. *"Only Luke is with me. Three words. One lifetime of faithfulness compressed."*
4. *"Luke stayed. So can you. Not in your own strength. In the strength of the One who stayed for you."*

### The Central Preaching Contrast
| Demas | Luke |
|---|---|
| Loved the present age (*agapēsas ton nun aiōna*) | Loved the present Christ |
| Left when it became costly | Stayed when it became lonely |
| Drifted inwardly while working outwardly | Anchored inwardly while serving outwardly |
| Remembered for where he went (Thessalonica) | Remembered for where he stayed (Rome) |
| The word "forsaken" describes him | The word "only" describes him |

### Critical Preaching Cautions
- **Do not villainize Demas.** The congregation must see themselves in him — not distance from him. He was a fellow laborer, not an apostate. The warning is the proximity.
- **The interior turn must arrive before the prescription.** If you move to "here is what to do" before you have sat with "here is who we are," the sermon becomes a lecture.
- **Do not over-taxonomize the diagnostic section.** Three diagnoses is the maximum. Each needs space to breathe, not just a label.
- **Let Luke's "only" breathe.** Do not rush past it. That word is the emotional center of the sermon.
- **The close is an invitation, not a verdict.** The tone shifts from diagnostic to pastoral in the final minutes.

## Opening and Closing Moments

### Recommended Opening (no warm-up — drop them in)
Begin mid-scene: Paul. Roman prison. Second imprisonment — not house arrest. Cold cell. Execution approaching. He writes what will be his last letter. And at the end of that letter, he takes attendance.

Read 2 Timothy 4:9–11 aloud. Slowly. Do not comment yet. Let it sit.

*Then:* "Two names. Two men. Same mission field. Same pressures. Same world pulling at both of them. One stayed. The other drifted."

### Recommended Closing
Return to the scene. Not Paul's cell — but this room. This Sabbath. This congregation leaving for the world again in a few hours.

Read Revelation 2:4–5 quietly. Then: "Remember. Repent. Return. Christ does not say to the church at Ephesus: *work harder*. He says: *love Me first*."

Final line: *"Luke stayed. So can you. Not in your own strength. In the strength of the One who stayed for you."*

## Congregational Application Points (mark these for eye contact)

1. **The urgency question:** "When did you last feel the urgency you felt the first time you understood what the gospel actually meant?"
2. **The love question:** "What would you struggle to surrender if Christ asked for it this week?"
3. **The trajectory question:** "You will leave this building today and return to the *aiōn* — the present age. Which man are you becoming?"
4. **The 'only' question:** "Is there someone whose faithfulness is their *only* right now — and you are not showing up?"
