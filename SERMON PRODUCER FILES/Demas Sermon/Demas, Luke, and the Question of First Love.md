There is something quietly powerful in the figure of Demas. He is not introduced as a villain, but as a co-laborer. And that is what makes his story so instructive, especially for a mature congregation and for young adults navigating faith in a distracted age. You have already touched this vein in your prior work: **Demas did not leave the work first. He left his love first.** This sermon can deepen that insight by placing Demas alongside Luke, and ultimately alongside Christ.

What emerges is not just a warning, but a call to return.

## Sermon Title

**“Demas, Luke, and the Question of First Love”**

## Core Texts

* 2 Timothy 4:10
* Colossians 4:14
* Philemon 1:24
* Revelation 2:4-5
* Luke 9:23

---

## Opening (3–5 minutes): The Quiet Tragedy of Demas

Begin with a reflective tone:

Demas is first mentioned not in failure, but in fellowship. In Philemon 1:24, he is called a “fellow laborer.” In Colossians 4:14, he is listed alongside Luke, “the beloved physician.”

Then comes the final note in 2 Timothy 4:10:

> “Demas hath forsaken me, having loved this present world…”

You might pause here and let the weight settle.

**Key idea:**
This is not a sudden fall. It is a slow drift of affection.

Bring in your prior language:

* “Demas did not wake up one morning and abandon Paul. He first loosened his grip on Christ.”

---

## Movement I (7–8 minutes): The Drift of Demas

### Scripture Focus

2 Timothy 4:10

### Development

Demas represents a **gradual spiritual erosion**:

* He stayed near the work but drifted from the heart of the work
* He maintained proximity to ministry but lost intimacy with Christ

### Contrast Insight

The issue was not persecution alone
The text says: *“having loved this present world”*

This is not merely fear
It is misplaced affection

### Ellen G. White

Introduce Ellen G. White:

> “It is not the open and avowed enemies of Christ that are most to be feared… but those who, like Demas, have loved this present world.”
> (*Adapted from* Acts of the Apostles)

### Application (Diaspora sensitivity)

You can gently connect to your earlier diaspora reflections:

* The world offers security, status, and survival
* Especially for immigrants, professionals, and strivers

But the danger is subtle:

* “We build lives, but lose our center”
* “We gain the world, but slowly lose the warmth of devotion”

---

## Movement II (7–8 minutes): The Faithfulness of Luke

### Scripture Focus

Colossians 4:14
2 Timothy 4:11

> “Only Luke is with me.”

Luke stays when Demas leaves.

### Development

Luke is not loud in Scripture
He is steady

He represents:

* Quiet endurance
* Faithfulness without recognition
* Commitment when the cost rises

### Contrast Structure

| Demas                      | Luke                         |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Loved the present world    | Loved the present Christ     |
| Left when it became costly | Stayed when it became lonely |
| Drifted inwardly           | Anchored inwardly            |

### Ellen G. White

> “God does not estimate a man’s character by the amount of work he does, but by the spirit in which the work is done.”

Luke stayed not because it was easy, but because his love remained intact.

---

## Movement III (7–8 minutes): The Root Issue — First Love

### Scripture Focus

Revelation 2:4-5

> “Thou hast left thy first love…”

This is where you unify the sermon.

Demas is not an isolated case
He is a living illustration of Ephesus

### Development

Christ’s concern is not activity
It is affection

A person can:

* Preach and yet drift
* Serve and yet cool
* Lead and yet lose love

### Christ’s Prescription

From the text:

* **Remember**
* **Repent**
* **Return**

This is deeply pastoral and fits your devotional tone well.

---

## Movement IV (5–6 minutes): The Call of Christ

### Scripture Focus

Luke 9:23

> “If any man will come after me, let him deny himself…”

Bring in Luke again here.
The Gospel of Luke records the cost of discipleship.

Now the contrast sharpens:

* Demas loved the present world
* Christ calls us to deny the present self

### Integration Insight

Luke (the writer) preserves the words
Luke (the companion) lives the words

---

## Closing (3–5 minutes): A Gentle but Urgent Appeal

This is where your voice can become quietly prophetic.

You are not asking:
“Have you left the church?”

You are asking:
**“Has your love shifted?”**

### Final Reflection

* Where has affection drifted?
* What has slowly replaced devotion?
* What once burned that is now dim?

### Ellen G. White Closing Thought

> “The greatest want of the world is the want of men… who will stand for the right though the heavens fall.”

### Final Line (Strong, memorable)

“Demas walked with Paul, but Luke walked with Christ. And in the end, only one remained.”

---

## Structural Summary for Delivery

You now have a natural 30-minute flow:

1. **Introduction – Demas the Companion (5 min)**
2. **The Drift – Love of the World (8 min)**
3. **The Contrast – Luke’s Faithfulness (8 min)**
4. **The Diagnosis – Loss of First Love (6–7 min)**
5. **The Call – Return to Christ (5 min)**

---

This framework preserves your earlier devotional insight while expanding it into a full, theologically grounded sermon. It also carries your signature tone: reflective, pastoral, and quietly searching rather than forceful.
