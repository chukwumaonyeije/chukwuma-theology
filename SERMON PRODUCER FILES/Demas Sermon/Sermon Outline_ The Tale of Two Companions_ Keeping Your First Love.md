# Sermon Outline: The Tale of Two Companions: Keeping Your First Love

**Text:** 2 Timothy 4:9-11; Revelation 2:4-5

**Thesis:** The Christian journey is not merely about how we start, but how we finish; while Demas traded eternal riches for the temporary comforts of this present world by losing his first love, Luke remained steadfast through the darkest trials, demonstrating that true faith is anchored in a continuous, abiding love for Christ.

---

## Introduction (5 minutes)

**Hook:** Start with a reflection on the concept of "drifting." When a boat drifts, it doesn't happen violently or suddenly. It happens slowly, imperceptibly, when the anchor is no longer secured. In our spiritual lives, no one wakes up one morning and decides to abandon their faith. It is a slow fade—a gradual exchange of the eternal for the temporary. This is the story of two men who started in the exact same place but ended up on completely different shores.

**Introduce the Problem/Tension:** In the modern church, especially among young adults and the diaspora, we face the subtle but powerful pull of worldly ambition. The pressure to succeed, to accumulate wealth, to gain honor, and to secure our comfort often competes with our devotion to God. We are tempted to become like the church of Ephesus in Revelation 2—doing all the right things, holding all the right doctrines, yet slowly losing our "first love."

**Introduce the Text:** Read 2 Timothy 4:9-11 aloud. "Do thy diligence to come shortly unto me: For Demas hath forsaken me, having loved this present world, and is departed unto Thessalonica... Only Luke is with me." Then read Revelation 2:4-5 aloud. "Nevertheless I have somewhat against thee, because thou hast left thy first love."

**State the Thesis:** The Christian journey is not merely about how we start, but how we finish. While Demas traded eternal riches for the temporary comforts of this present world by losing his first love, Luke remained steadfast through the darkest trials, demonstrating that true faith is anchored in a continuous, abiding love for Christ.

**Roadmap:** This morning at Braselton Seventh-day Adventist Church, we will explore three profound truths from the lives of these two companions: the danger of a divided heart, the tragedy of the shortsighted exchange, and the beauty of steadfast faithfulness.

---

## Point 1: The Danger of a Divided Heart (10 minutes)

**Explanation:** Demas was not a casual observer; he was a genuine convert and a fellow laborer with the Apostle Paul. In Philemon 1:24 and Colossians 4:14, Paul lists Demas alongside Luke as a faithful worker. He had seen the miracles, heard the preaching, and endured the early struggles. Yet, Paul writes from a cold, damp Roman dungeon that Demas has forsaken him. Why? Because he "loved this present world." The Greek word for world here is *aion*, referring to the current age—its systems, its values, its safety, and its allure. Demas did not necessarily become a pagan overnight; his heart simply shifted its affection. This perfectly mirrors the warning to the church of Ephesus in Revelation 2: they had left their first love. Ellen G. White notes, "The first affection of the convert to Christ is deep, full, and ardent... God sees that there is not heart service, a love for Jesus, an earnest zeal in his work" [1].

**Illustration:** Compare this to a marriage. A marriage doesn't usually collapse because of a sudden catastrophic event, but because the daily, intentional acts of love and connection are replaced by routine, distraction, and a love for other things (career, hobbies, status). The affection cools.

**Application:** We must examine our own hearts today. Have we allowed the love of "this present world"—our careers, our social status, our financial security—to slowly replace our first love for Jesus? Are we serving God out of habit while our hearts belong to the world? We must guard against the subtle drift of worldliness in the church.

---

## Point 2: The Tragedy of the Shortsighted Exchange (10 minutes)

**Explanation:** When Paul was imprisoned in Rome under Nero, it was no longer safe to be a Christian. To associate with Paul was to invite suspicion, poverty, and potentially death. Demas looked at the cost of discipleship and decided the price was too high. He sought the ease and security of Thessalonica. He made a trade. Ellen G. White captures this tragedy powerfully: "For worldly gain, Demas bartered every high and noble consideration. How shortsighted the exchange! Possessing only worldly wealth or honor, Demas was poor indeed, however much he might proudly call his own" [2]. He traded eternal glory for temporary comfort.

**Illustration:** Imagine a person who sells a winning lottery ticket worth millions for a single, cheap meal because they are momentarily hungry. The meal satisfies for an hour, but the loss is permanent. Demas sold out his eternal inheritance for the fleeting safety of a worldly life.

**Application:** What are we trading our spiritual birthright for? In our pursuit of the "American Dream," professional accolades, or societal approval, are we bartering away our high and noble calling? We must remember that possessing worldly wealth or honor without Christ makes us "poor indeed." True wealth is found in maintaining our covenantal relationship with God, even when it costs us earthly comfort.

---

## Point 3: The Beauty of Steadfast Faithfulness (10 minutes)

**Explanation:** In stark contrast to Demas stands Luke, "the beloved physician." While others fled the thickening clouds of danger, Luke remained. Paul writes, "Only Luke is with me." Luke was a man of education, a physician who could have easily secured a comfortable, lucrative practice anywhere in the Roman Empire. Yet, he chose the damp, dark vaults of a Roman prison to minister to an aging, chained apostle. Why? Because his first love remained intact. Ellen G. White writes of this time, "The services of Luke, the beloved disciple and faithful friend, were a great comfort to Paul and enabled him to communicate with his brethren and the world without" [3]. Luke understood that to win Christ was infinite gain, and to win the world at the cost of the soul was infinite loss.

**Illustration:** Think of the anchor of a ship in a storm. While the winds howl and the waves crash, the anchor holds fast, unseen beneath the surface, gripping the solid rock. Luke’s faith was anchored in Christ. The storm of Nero's persecution could not dislodge him because his love for the Savior was deeper than his fear of the world.

**Application:** We are called to be the "Lukes" of our generation. When the church faces trials, when following Christ becomes unpopular or costly, will we remain steadfast? As a physician and a believer, I see every day that our true calling is not just professional success, but to be faithful companions in the gospel. We must renew our commitment to Christ daily, ensuring that our love for Him burns brighter than any worldly ambition.

---

## Conclusion (5 minutes)

**Summarize Main Points:** We have seen the danger of a divided heart that slowly drifts away from its first love. We have witnessed the tragic, shortsighted exchange of Demas, who traded eternal riches for temporary worldly comfort. And we have been inspired by the steadfast faithfulness of Luke, the beloved physician, who remained anchored in Christ through the darkest trial.

**Restate Thesis:** The true measure of our faith is not how enthusiastically we begin, but how faithfully we endure. We must choose whether we will be defined by a love for this present world, or by an abiding, sacrificial love for our Savior.

**Call to Action:** Today, I challenge you to examine your heart. Have you left your first love? Have the cares of this present world dimmed your zeal for God? Jesus says to us, as He said to Ephesus: "Remember therefore from whence thou art fallen, and repent, and do the first works" (Rev 2:5). Let us recommit our lives to Him today. Let us choose the path of Luke—the path of steadfast faithfulness, no matter the cost.

**Closing Prayer:** Father in Heaven, we thank You for the stark contrast between Demas and Luke, which serves as a warning and an inspiration to us. Forgive us for the times we have allowed the love of this present world to eclipse our love for You. Rekindle our first love. Give us the courage and the steadfastness of Luke to remain faithful to You, even when the world pulls at our hearts. May we be found faithful when You return. In Jesus' name, Amen.
