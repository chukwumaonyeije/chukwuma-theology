# Day 3 of 7 — Point 1 Delivery Practice
## "God Meets Us at Empty, Not After Empty" (1 Kings 19:3–7)
**Target: 10–12 minutes | Today: Rehearse aloud twice. Time it.**

---

## SPEAKING OUTLINE (bullet points — not for reading, for triggering)

**ENTRY MOVE** — Don't announce the point. Drop into the scene.
- Elijah just called fire from heaven. Jezebel just sent a death threat.
- One letter. That's all it takes. The prophet runs.
- He doesn't stop at the city limits. He goes a day's journey into the wilderness — alone.
- He sits down. Under a juniper tree.
- And he says: *"It is enough."*

**Sub-point A — The Prayer Under the Juniper Tree (v.4)**
- *Rav* — the Hebrew word here — doesn't mean "I'm a little low"
  - It means: the cup has been filled past its capacity. It is spilling.
  - Pain is running over the edges.
- Notice what Elijah does NOT do: he does not go silent, does not stop praying
  - He is broken — but he is still TALKING TO GOD
  - This is a crisis of capacity, not a crisis of faith
  - He is still in the conversation
- Pause here. Let that land.
- The man under the tree is not a backslider. He is a burned-out believer.

**Sub-point B — What God Does NOT Do**
- This is where you slow down. Take your time. Every "no" is a gift.
- God does NOT:
  - Rebuke him — (no "you should know better")
  - Remind him of his calling — (no "remember Carmel?")
  - Replay his victories — (no "look what I did through you")
  - Assign a new task — (no "get back to work")
  - Tell him to pray harder — (no "you just need more faith")
- Five things a preacher would do. God does none of them.
- Pause. Full stop.
- What does heaven look like when you're under your juniper tree?

**Sub-point C — What God DOES**
- Sends an angel
  - Not an angel with a sermon. With bread. And water.
  - "Arise and eat." That's the whole message.
- Then — full sleep. Let the body rest.
- Then a second touch. *Gentle.* Not urgent.
  - Notice: the angel doesn't say "you've slept enough"
  - The angel touches him *again* — tenderness, not impatience
- Then — and only then — the diagnosis:
  - *"The journey is too great for thee."*
  - God NAMES the limitation. He doesn't spiritualize it.
  - He doesn't say: "You lack vision." He says: "You are tired. The road is long."

**PIVOT TO ILLUSTRATION** — [HELLP transition — see below]

**LANDING THE POINT**
- Pullquote — say it slowly, let the room hold it:
  - *"God's first response to your emptiness is not a new assignment. It is a meal."*
- Brief SDA thread — don't rush it, but keep it tight:
  - Sabbath is not a reward for finishing your work
  - God built rest into the architecture of creation on Day 7
  - Not because He was tired — because He was showing us: you were never designed to run without stopping
  - Elijah received the Sabbath principle in emergency deployment
- Close Point 1 with a landing question for the congregation:
  - "What if the most spiritual thing you can do today is sleep?"

## HELLP ILLUSTRATION — WITH TRANSITION LANGUAGE

### ENTERING THE MEDICAL STORY
> *"Let me put you in a room with me for a moment."*

You're a physician. It's 2 a.m. A woman comes in — she's pregnant, she's swollen, her blood pressure is dangerously high. Her eyes are frightened. Her nausea is written all over her face. The labs come back and you see the triad: hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, low platelets. This is HELLP syndrome — a life-threatening complication of severe preeclampsia. She is heading toward seizure. Toward stroke. Toward liver injury.

**What do you do first?**

You do not pull up a chair and say: *"Let me explain what you should have done during the last eight months of this pregnancy."*

You stabilize. Lower the blood pressure. Start magnesium to protect her brain. Protect the mother. Assess the baby.

The question is never *"Who's to blame?"* The question is: *"What must be done right now?"*

### EXITING THE MEDICAL STORY AND LANDING IN THE TEXT
> *"God is the best clinician in the room."*

When Elijah arrives at that juniper tree, God does not conduct a review. He does not open the chart of Elijah's ministry failures. He does not ask for a report.

He stabilizes first. He commissions later.

And here is the word for someone in this room today who has been waiting for God to rebuke you before He restores you — you may be waiting for something God is not going to do.

**Where to slow down most:**
1. *"This is a crisis of capacity, not a crisis of faith."* — say it, pause, say it again differently
2. The list of what God does NOT do — one beat between each item
3. *"The journey is too great for thee."* — let the congregation sit with God naming the limitation

**Where to pause (full stop):**
- After: *"He is still in the conversation."*
- After the full list of five things God doesn't do
- After the pullquote at the end

## THREE POTENTIAL STUMBLE POINTS + RECOVERY

### Stumble 1: Over-explaining the Hebrew (*rav*)
**The risk:** You go deep on the word study and lose the room, or spend 3 minutes on etymology.
**Recovery:** One sentence only — *"The Hebrew word there, rav, means the cup isn't just low — it's spilling over."* Then move. If you went too long, don't apologize; just pick up the pace on Sub-point B.

### Stumble 2: The "what God doesn't do" list loses energy or becomes a lecture
**The risk:** You list the five things mechanically and the weight of each one doesn't land.
**Recovery:** If you feel the room going flat, stop the list, make eye contact, and say: *"Five things. Any one of them we might have done. God did none of them."* Then let the silence work. Re-anchor with the pivot: *"So what did God do?"*

### Stumble 3: The HELLP illustration runs clinical instead of pastoral
**The risk:** You get into medical detail (platelets, magnesium dosing) and lose the emotional arc.
**Recovery:** If you realize you're in the weeds, cut immediately to the principle: *"Bottom line — you stabilize first. God stabilized first."* Don't back up. Bridge forward and exit into the text.
