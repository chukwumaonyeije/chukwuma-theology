# Day 5 of 7 — Point 3 Delivery Practice
## "God Commissions Broken People" (1 Kings 19:13–18)
**Target: 8–10 minutes | Today: Rehearse Point 3 aloud twice. The stillbirth story should feel like a human moment, not a sermon illustration. If it feels forced, slow down.**

---

## SPEAKING OUTLINE (bullet points — not for reading, for triggering)

**ENTRY MOVE** — The repetition is the point. Do not rush past it.
- Verse 13. God comes again. Same question.
- *"What are you doing here, Elijah?"*
- Word for word. Same tone. Same question.
- And Elijah gives the same answer. Word for word. *He has not moved.*
- He is not fixed. He has not had a breakthrough. The bread and sleep did not cure the wound.
- And God does not say: *"We covered this."*
- He asks again. Because Elijah is still here. And the conversation is not finished just because the patient has not changed yet.

**PIVOT TO STILLBIRTH STORY** — [see full section below — handle with care]

**Sub-point A — God Repeats the Question (v.13)**
- The second asking is not redundancy. It is reassurance.
- It is God saying: *I am still in the room. I have not moved on.*
- In medicine: a patient who is not improving may need the same intervention with more time — or they may need something entirely new. You do not abandon the intake; you deepen it.
- What changes between verse 9 and verse 13: *the fact that God asked again.*
- The question itself is the ministry.
- Elijah still believes the same wrong things. God does not yet correct them.
  - Not because the correction is wrong.
  - Because there is a sequence to care. And correction is not the first move.

**Sub-point B — The Assignment (vv.15–17)**
- Then something shifts. Not rebuke. Commission.
- *Go back the way you came.*
  - The way you came. The road that broke you. You are going back on it.
- Anoint Hazael — king of Syria.
- Anoint Jehu — king of Israel.
- Anoint Elisha — your own successor.
- Three real, geopolitically consequential tasks.
  - Not symbolic. Not therapeutic. *Historical.*
  - Nations will turn on these acts.
- God does not say: *When you're better, there is work for you.*
- God says: *Here is the work. Go.*
- The commission comes before the complete healing.
  - The assignment comes while the wound is still fresh.
  - Grace does not require full restoration before it trusts you again.
- Pause here. Let this be the fulcrum.
  - *God does not bench His broken people. He deploys them.*

**Sub-point C — The Correction God Finally Gives — Gently (v.18)**
- **[The seven thousand — see full section below — do not rush this moment]**
- *"Yet I have reserved seven thousand in Israel, all the knees which have not bowed unto Baal."*
- This is the only correction in the whole encounter.
  - Not in chapter 19, verse 4 under the juniper tree — no correction there.
  - Not in verse 9 at the mouth of the cave — no correction there.
  - Not in verse 13 — no correction there.
  - *Here.* After the bread. After the sleep. After the presence. After the wind and earthquake and fire. After the assignment.
- God earns the right to correct by caring first.
- And when the correction comes, it is not a rebuke.
  - It is a revelation.
  - *"You thought you were the only one. You are not."*
- Seven thousand knees that have not bowed to Baal.
  - Invisible to Elijah. Quiet. Uncelebrated. Unknown to each other, perhaps.
  - They did not do what Carmel did. They simply did not bow.
  - And God has seen every one of them.

**REMNANT THEOLOGY — brief, 3–4 sentences, woven into the seven thousand**
- The remnant is not a boastful category. It exists by grace.
- They are not preserved because they are strong. They are preserved because God is faithful.
- Isolation felt real to Elijah — but the reality it pointed to was false.
- The enemy's tool: not to make you doubt God directly, but to make you doubt the existence of any community worth trusting.

**SANCTUARY CLOSE — [see full section below — brief, high, intercessory]**

**PULLQUOTE — land this as the capstone of the point, not the transition to Point 4**
> *"God commissions broken people. That has always been His method. Not because brokenness is ideal — but because it is honest. And honest is exactly where God does His best work."*
- Say it slowly. Both sentences need room.
- Don't move to the next beat for three full seconds after the last word.

## THE STILLBIRTH STORY — FULL DELIVERY GUIDANCE

> **HANDLE WITH CARE.** This is the most vulnerable personal disclosure in the entire sermon. It is not a transition device. It is not sermon machinery. It is a human moment. Deliver it as one.

### WHEN TO ENTER
Enter the story coming *out of* the second asking in Sub-point A — specifically after the line: *"The question itself is the ministry."* The story illuminates why the second asking matters more than the first.

### ENTERING THE STORY
Do not announce it as an illustration. Do not say *"Let me tell you a story."* Just step into it.

> *"I need to tell you something personal."*

Pause. Let that phrase land. The congregation will feel the register change. Let them.

> *"Before I was born, my mother — Cecilia Onyeije — had a stillbirth."*

Stop. Do not add anything immediately. Let the sentence exist by itself for a moment.

> *"She buried a baby before she had me."*

---

### PACING THROUGH THE STORY
What follows is not an anecdote with a punchline. It is a slow walk.

- Speak in short sentences. No complex constructions here.
- If you feel your voice tighten, do not try to clear it. Let it be what it is.
- The congregation needs to feel that you feel this — not that you are performing having felt it.

> *"When you are a mother who has buried a baby — and then you carry another pregnancy — the fear does not leave. It changes shape. It gets quiet. But it doesn't leave.*
>
> *Every scan. Every heartbeat check. Every moment in the triage bay or the exam room when a clinician leans toward the monitor and takes a second too long before speaking.*
>
> *For that mother, reassurance is not simply information.*
>
> *It is mercy.*"

### THE CONNECTION TO THE TEXT
This is the bridge. Say it gently, not theatrically.

> *"God asking Elijah the same question a second time — using the same words, the same tone, in the same cave — that second asking is not redundancy.*
>
> *It is what the clinician does when she turns to the mother a second time and says: I'm still here. I am still looking. I have not left the room.*
>
> *The second asking is where the soul begins to realize: God is still in the room. The conversation is not over simply because I have not gotten better yet."*

### EXITING THE STORY
Do not linger. Do not debrief it. Do not say *"isn't that beautiful."* Exit clean and let the story do its work.

Simply transition to Sub-point B: *"And then — God gives Elijah an assignment."*

The congregation needs a moment to breathe. Moving into the commission gives them forward motion while the personal story settles.

### WHAT TO WATCH FOR IN REHEARSAL
- If the story feels like an illustration, you are moving too fast.
- If you feel the urge to explain it or tie it up before you've finished it — resist that urge. Let the story be incomplete for a moment. Incompleteness is not a flaw; it is the sermon honoring the grief in the room.
- If grief moves through the room, let it. Do not preach over it.
- If you find yourself emotionally flooded and lose your place: take a breath, look up, and return to the text. *"God asks again"* is always your anchor.

## THE SEVEN THOUSAND — HOW TO LET IT LAND

This is the correction God has been building toward for nineteen verses. It must not be rushed.

### THE SETUP
Before you quote verse 18, let the silence set in. You've just described the commission — three real tasks, kingdoms turning. And Elijah is still a man who believes he is alone.

Say it plainly:

> *"Elijah has been saying the same thing since verse 10. 'I, even I only, am left.'*
>
> *He has been carrying that sentence like a stone. It has been in his chest on the forty-day road. In the cave. Through the wind and the earthquake and the fire. Through the commission.*
>
> *He still believes it."*

Pause.

> *"And then — finally — God responds to it."*

### QUOTING THE VERSE
Quote it slowly. Don't rush to the implications.

> *"Yet I have reserved seven thousand in Israel — all the knees which have not bowed unto Baal."*

Let it sit. Do not immediately explain it. Do not say "in other words."

Five full seconds. Look at the congregation. Let them do the math themselves.

### THE LANDING
Then, and only then:

> *"Seven thousand.*
>
> *Elijah did not know their names. He had never heard their stories. He could not point to a single one of them.*
>
> *They were not on the mountaintop at Carmel. They did not call fire from heaven. They are not in any highlight reel.*
>
> *They simply — did not bow.*
>
> *And God had counted every one of them."*

### WHAT NOT TO DO HERE
- Do not immediately pivot to application ("and maybe you feel like Elijah today"). That will come. But not yet. Let the number breathe.
- Do not turn the seven thousand into a sermon about the faithful remnant who are watching from the pews. That is a good sermon. It is not this moment. This moment is about Elijah's loneliness being — factually — wrong.
- Do not soften the correction: Elijah was wrong. Let the wrongness register first. Then move to the grace embedded in how God delivered the correction.

## THE SANCTUARY CLOSE — BRIEF AND HIGH

This paragraph should feel like the ceiling lifting. It is short by design — four to six sentences. It is not a new argument. It is a horizon.

Transition in from the remnant thread:

> *"And we do not come to this story alone.*
>
> *We have a High Priest — right now, actively — who is interceding on our behalf. Hebrews 4:15 says He was touched with the feeling of our infirmities. He was not a distant spectator of Elijah's juniper tree.*
>
> *He has been to Gethsemane. He knows what it is to be wrung out and ask for it to be over.*
>
> *He is not watching from a distance. He is in the room. The conversation is not finished. And He is still asking."*

Close Point 3 here. The pullquote follows immediately.

## TIMING GUIDANCE

| Section | Target Time | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Entry move (second question) | ~1 min | Spare — the repetition is the whole point. Trust it. |
| Sub-point A + stillbirth story | ~3 min | The story should be 90 seconds. If it runs 2 min, that is acceptable. Do not cut it short. |
| Sub-point B (the assignment) | ~2 min | Crisp. The three tasks should feel consequential, not rushed. |
| Sub-point C (seven thousand) | ~2 min | Slow on the number itself. Five-second pause after quoting. |
| Remnant thread + sanctuary close | ~1 min | High and brief. This is the lift before the landing. |
| Pullquote + close | ~30 sec | Don't rush it. The pullquote IS the point. |
| **TOTAL** | **~9.5 min** | |

**Where to slow down most:**
1. *"Before I was born, my mother had a stillbirth."* — say it and stop.
2. After quoting verse 18 — five full seconds of silence.
3. The pullquote — both sentences, deliberate, with air between them.

**Where the temptation to rush will be highest:**
- The stillbirth story (because it is vulnerable, and rushing is a way of protecting yourself)
- The seven thousand (because you'll want to get to the application)
- The transition out of the sanctuary paragraph (because you'll feel the pull to get to the conclusion)

Resist each of these. The momentum of the sermon is not an excuse to leave the congregation behind.

## THREE POTENTIAL STUMBLE POINTS + RECOVERY

### Stumble 1: The stillbirth story feels like an illustration instead of a testimony
**The risk:** You find yourself narrating it as a setup for the point about the second asking, and it loses its weight.
**Recovery:** If you feel it going flat mid-story, slow down physically. Take a pause. Lower your voice slightly. Say the name: *"Cecilia Onyeije."* Names anchor testimony. The name brings it back to a real person, which brings it back to you.

### Stumble 2: The seven thousand becomes a pep talk too quickly
**The risk:** You quote verse 18 and immediately move to "maybe you feel alone today," bypassing the actual correction landing.
**Recovery:** After quoting the verse, do not say anything for a deliberate beat. Count it in your head if you need to — three counts, five counts. The silence tells the congregation that what they just heard is worth sitting in. Then say: *"Elijah was not the only one. He had simply stopped being able to see."* That keeps the focus on Elijah before you bring it to them.

### Stumble 3: The sanctuary close runs long and dilutes the pullquote
**The risk:** The sanctuary paragraph is so rich that you keep adding to it, and by the time you reach the pullquote it is buried.
**Recovery:** The sanctuary close has a hard ceiling: six sentences. When you reach *"He is still asking"* — stop. Do not add a seventh sentence. Move directly to the pullquote. The pullquote is the destination. Everything else is the road.
