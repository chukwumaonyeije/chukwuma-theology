## Executive Summary
These exegesis files read 1 Kings 19 as a chapter about divine pursuit, correction, and restoration at the edge of prophetic collapse. The central insight is that God's repeated question to Elijah is diagnostic rather than cruel, exposing the prophet's wounded narrative while refusing to leave him inside it. The package is firmly Adventist in tone, framing the chapter through remnant theology, Great Controversy logic, and the call back into mission.

## Concepts
- Chapter 19 must be read against the violent contrast with chapter 18, where public triumph is followed by private collapse.
- Verses 1-3 trace the anatomy of fear; verses 4-8 show God restoring the whole person; verses 9-14 interpret the cave and the quiet voice; verses 15-21 restore mission and widen Elijah's vision of the remnant.
- Horeb matters because the text places Elijah inside covenant memory, not just inside personal burnout.
- The still small voice is treated as a mode of divine self-disclosure that corrects spectacle without denying God's power.

## Insights
- The files consistently refuse to reduce Elijah to a clinical case study, even while making room for emotional exhaustion and distorted perception.
- The remnant correction is one of the most important theological turns in the chapter: Elijah says he is alone, and God answers with unseen faithfulness.
