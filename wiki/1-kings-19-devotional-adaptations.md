## Executive Summary
These two devotional drafts split 1 Kings 19 into a family-facing sequence: God's care under the broom tree and God's voice at Horeb. Both drafts retain the physician-pastor tone of the larger Elijah project while compressing it into direct, evening-scale reflection. Together they show how the sermon material can be adapted without losing either tenderness or theological substance.

## Concepts
- Part one centers on sleep, food, touch, and the mercy of God's embodied care.
- Part two centers on divine quietness, repeated questioning, and commission after lament.
- Clinical analogies are retained, but only as brief bridges that help families notice the difference between drama and diagnosis.
- Each devotional ends with prompts that move the passage from exposition into household use.

## Insights
- The adaptations work because they preserve the sermon's core movement while reducing scale, technical detail, and argumentative weight.
- The pair should remain linked because verses 1-8 and verses 9-18 together form the full restoration arc.
- These files are downstream applications of [[when-the-tank-is-not-low-its-empty|When the Tank Is Not Low. It's Empty]] and should usually be read after that primary note.
