## Executive Summary
This source cluster builds a full sermon ecosystem around the contrast between Demas and Luke in Paul's closing ministry context. Its central claim is that the decisive difference between the two men is not talent or access to ministry, but the location of their love. The package consistently moves from narrative contrast to spiritual diagnosis and ends with Christ's call to remember, repent, and return.

## Concepts
- Demas is treated as a case study in gradual drift rather than spectacular apostasy.
- Luke functions as the quiet counterexample: a professional, a witness, and the one who stayed when Paul's circle thinned.
- Revelation 2:4-5 supplies the sermon's interpretive key by naming lost first love as the root disorder behind outward religious activity.
- Ellen White references are used to connect Demas, Laodicean self-satisfaction, and the difference between forms of religion and their animating spirit.

## Insights
- The sermon architecture works because it starts with Paul's last roll call, reads Demas across all three New Testament mentions, and only then turns toward present-day application.
- "Good work replaces first love" is the most reusable line in the file set because it translates ancient drift into ministry ambition, career success, digital noise, and respectable busyness.
- The Luke material is not sentimental; it deliberately frames fidelity as embodied discipleship that remains present in cold, inconvenient, and unglamorous places.
- The strongest closing movement is diagnostic rather than condemnatory, which makes this note a natural companion to [[demas-and-the-drift|Demas and the Drift]] and [[fellowship-of-the-finish|Fellowship of the Finish]].

- Primary biblical anchors: 2 Timothy 4:9-11, Philemon 1:24, Colossians 4:14, Revelation 2:4-5, and Luke 9:23.
- Supporting research and quotations emphasize Ephesus, Laodicea, and Luke's faithfulness during Paul's imprisonment.
