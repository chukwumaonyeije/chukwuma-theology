## Executive Summary
This cluster reframes the Demas material as an interactive vespers experience that asks participants where Christ sits in the economy of their attention. The files repeatedly argue that drift happens when respectable responsibilities displace first love without announcing themselves as rebellion. The design relies on silence, live polling, and guided discussion to move the audience from analysis of Demas toward self-diagnosis.

## Concepts
- The event is structured around progressive honesty: prayer, polling, biblical observation, diagnosis, and practical response.
- Demas is not positioned as a cautionary tale for "other people" but as a mirror for high-functioning believers whose work and ministry may be displacing communion with Christ.
- "Good work vs. first love" is the governing tension across the facilitator copy, script, and slides.
- The vespers format favors short statements, pauses, and room participation instead of dense exposition.

## Insights
- The strongest pastoral move in the script is the opening line: "Tonight is not about judging Demas. It is about recognizing how close we are to him."
- The live prompts translate theology into inventory questions about attention, ambition, comfort, and future trajectory.
- This note belongs in `sermon-design` rather than `discipleship` because the source set is primarily a facilitation package, even though its core burden overlaps with [[two-men-one-gospel|Two Men, One Gospel]].
- The materials are especially reusable for young adult ministry, retreat settings, and diagnostic Sabbath evening conversations.
