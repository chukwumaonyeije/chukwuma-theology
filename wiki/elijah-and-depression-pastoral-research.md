## Executive Summary
This research bundle gathers outside language for speaking about exhaustion, distorted thinking, fear, isolation, and recovery in the Elijah narrative. Across the sources, the most stable pastoral pattern is that God responds to collapse with embodied care, patient questioning, renewed perspective, and restored mission. The files are useful as a pastoral bridge, but they repeatedly need the doctrinal ballast supplied by [[1-kings-19-exegesis|1 Kings 19 Exegesis]] so that the sermon remains theological rather than merely therapeutic.

## Concepts
- High spiritual intensity is often followed by vulnerable emotional troughs.
- Recurrent signs of collapse include fear, withdrawal, irrational interpretations, and inability to sustain normal resilience.
- God's early response to Elijah is concrete before it is corrective: sleep, food, touch, and presence precede argument.
- The repeated question "What are you doing here?" functions as diagnostic invitation rather than divine mockery.

## Insights
- These sources are strongest when they describe patterns of burnout with compassion and weakest when they risk flattening the text into a generic mental-health lesson.
- The cluster helps translate Elijah's experience into clinically literate and pastorally tender language that can serve congregations dealing with depletion.
- The note is best paired with [[when-the-tank-is-not-low-its-empty|When the Tank Is Not Low. It's Empty]] for proclamation and with [[1-kings-19-devotional-adaptations|1 Kings 19 Devotional Adaptations]] for family-facing application.
