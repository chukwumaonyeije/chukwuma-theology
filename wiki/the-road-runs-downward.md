## Executive Summary
This essay reads Mark 9:30-50 as a sustained argument about greatness delivered on the road to Jesus' death. It claims that the disciples' fear, ranking instincts, gatekeeping habits, and difficulty with judgment all become intelligible once the cross is seen as the interpretive center of the passage. The road runs downward because Christ walks it first, redefining greatness as self-giving descent rather than competitive ascent.

## Concepts
- Fear can masquerade as ignorance when the truth being avoided threatens cherished plans.
- Jesus' use of the child exposes the emptiness of status contests and relocates greatness in receptive service.
- Gatekeeping is treated as a symptom of insecure discipleship rather than a sign of mature spiritual authority.
- The difficult material on Gehenna, fire, and salt is read through Adventist concerns about judgment, purification, and finality.

## Insights
- The essay's recurring contrast between worldly ascent and Christ's downward road gives the whole piece coherence.
- Clinical observation is used sparingly but effectively to explain willful not-knowing and defensive avoidance.
